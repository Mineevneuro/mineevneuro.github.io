Automated lumbar pedicle screw planning

Can a simple geometric planner put lumbar pedicle screw trajectories where a surgeon would? Lumbar CT in, one trajectory per pedicle out, checked against my own manual plans.

Data
VerSe'19, open spine CT with vertebra masks
Scope
Lumbar spine, L1–L5
Planned stack
Python, NumPy, SciPy (distance transform); TotalSegmentator for masks on new scans
Status
Planned as my CS50x final project

Research and educational project. Not a medical device and not for clinical use.

Clinical context

In my guided cases the screws were planned by hand: segment the vertebra, find the narrowest part of the pedicle, set the entry point and angle, check the length. Trajectories and guide design in Blender took about 40 minutes per case. The trajectory part is geometry a program can do.

Plan

  1. Lumbar CT
  2. Vertebra masks
  3. Pedicle isthmus
  4. Trajectory
  5. trajectory-plan.json
  • Masks: VerSe ground-truth masks first, then TotalSegmentator to see how mask errors carry into the plan.
  • Pedicle: find the isthmus, the narrowest cross-section of the pedicle, with a distance transform.
  • Trajectory: a line through the isthmus centre into the vertebral body, with screw diameter and length chosen from the pedicle width and the body depth.
  • Output: a JSON plan, the same format as the trajectory-planner module of spine-unit-lab.

How it will be judged

Against manual plans I make on the same vertebrae: entry-point distance in millimetres and angle in degrees. These are the same metrics as in the guided screw accuracy analysis. The planned trajectory must also stay inside the pedicle.

Limits

  • One reference planner, me. A second surgeon's plans would show how much surgeons disagree with each other.
  • Lumbar spine only in the first version; thoracic pedicles are narrower and harder.
